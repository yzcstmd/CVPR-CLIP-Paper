\begin{abstract}
Adapting CLIP to ordinal classification, such as age or image-quality estimation, rarely comes with labeled images for every grade, and grades unseen during adaptation can disappear from the model's predictions. We show that an unseen grade poses two distinct problems: how often it is predicted, and how its images are ordered against neighboring grades. Class-bias calibration restores predictions but provably cannot change this pairwise order. Blending toward the zero-shot model can change the order, but only along a single path of adaptation strengths. Writing adapted scores as a zero-shot reference plus a learned displacement explains the gap: a missing grade's displacement is never fitted to its own images, but its ordinal neighbors' displacements are fitted to visually similar ones. We propose TIE, a training-free ordinal displacement transfer that inherits adaptation from neighboring seen grades while keeping each missing grade's own zero-shot reference. TIE preserves seen-grade scores and exactly restores the zero-shot margin between a missing endpoint and its nearest seen grade. Across four benchmarks, nine adapter configurations, and six missing-grade patterns, TIE with calibration lowers missing-grade and overall error over calibrated adapters on every benchmark and improves missing--seen ranking in 34 of 36 adapter--dataset settings. In a controlled comparison on interior grades that changes only where displacement comes from, TIE achieves ranking gains supported by paired 95\% intervals at 59 of 64 matched strengths. On three benchmarks, even the best of 17 shared blending strengths, selected on test data, falls short of TIE at its original strength. Ordinal neighbors thus supply what neither calibration nor global blending can: adaptation for grades that task training never saw.
\end{abstract}
