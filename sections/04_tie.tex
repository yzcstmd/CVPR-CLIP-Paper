\section{TIE: Ordinal Displacement Transfer}
\label{sec:tie}

\subsection{Neighbor Displacement Inheritance}

Section~\ref{sec:calibration_ranking} shows that shifting or globally scaling adapted scores cannot supply a missing grade with adaptation learned from its own images. TIE instead transfers displacement along the grade order. Adjacent grades often share visual cues, making their learned displacements a natural proxy for the adaptation the missing grade could not learn directly.

For each image $x$, let $T,S\in\mathbb R^K$ be the zero-shot and adapted scores, and let $D=S-T$. For a missing grade $k\in\mathcal U$, let $\ell(k)$ and $r(k)$ denote its nearest seen grades below and above it, when available. Writing $\ell=\ell(k)$ and $r=r(k)$, define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tie_operator}
[\mathcal L_{\mathcal U}(D)]_k=\begin{cases}
D_k, & k\in\mathcal O,\\
\tfrac12(D_{\ell}+D_r), & \ell,r\text{ exist},\\
D_{\ell}, & \ell\text{ only},\\
D_r, & r\text{ only}.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
The final scores are $S^{\mathrm{TIE}}=T+\mathcal L_{\mathcal U}(D)$. The rule handles individual missing grades and consecutive missing blocks. Its anchors follow the grade mask, and its weights are fixed.

TIE transfers the displacement, not the entire adapted score. Averaging neighboring scores would also replace $T_m$, the missing grade's own vision--language reference. TIE preserves that reference while borrowing the adaptation learned at neighboring grades.

\subsection{Structural Properties and Inheritance Error}

\begin{proposition}[Structure of TIE]
\label{prop:tie_structure}
For any missing set $\mathcal U$ and image $x$:
(i) \emph{seen scores are preserved}: $S_k^{\mathrm{TIE}}(x)=S_k(x)$ for all $k\in\mathcal O$;
(ii) \emph{endpoint margin recovery}: if $m$ is a missing endpoint of the grade space and $n$ its nearest seen grade, $g_{mn}^{S^{\mathrm{TIE}}}(x)=g_{mn}^T(x)$;
(iii) \emph{commutation with blending}: $T+\mathcal L_{\mathcal U}(\lambda D)=T+\lambda\mathcal L_{\mathcal U}(D)$ for all $\lambda\ge0$.
\end{proposition}
\noindent\emph{Proof.}
The operator copies seen coordinates, giving (i). At a missing endpoint, the displacement $D_n$ is inherited and cancels in the margin, giving (ii). Each output is a fixed linear combination of input coordinates, giving (iii).\hfill$\square$

These properties have direct consequences. Seen--seen comparisons remain exactly as adapted. The specified endpoint--neighbor margin returns to its zero-shot value. At matched $\lambda$, TIE and Blend share all seen-grade scores and differ only in the missing grades' displacement. Figure~\ref{fig:matched_strength} tests whether this transfer improves on the Blend family of Proposition~\ref{prop:family} (Sec.~\ref{sec:exp_matched}).

How close is inherited displacement to what a grade would learn with direct supervision? A full-support model provides a diagnostic reference $D^{\mathrm{FS}}=S^{\mathrm{FS}}-T$. For a held-out interior grade $m$ whose immediate neighbors $m-1,m+1$ are seen, define $\bar D_m=\tfrac12(D^{\mathrm{FS}}_{m-1}+D^{\mathrm{FS}}_{m+1})$.

\begin{proposition}[Transfer error is displacement curvature]
\label{prop:tie_error}
In this full-support diagnostic, the inheritance error $e_m(x)=D_m^{\mathrm{FS}}(x)-\bar D_m(x)$ is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tie_error}
\begin{aligned}
e_m(x)=-\tfrac12\bigl(&D_{m-1}^{\mathrm{FS}}(x)-2D_m^{\mathrm{FS}}(x)\\
&+D_{m+1}^{\mathrm{FS}}(x)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
For any seen grade $n$, the full-support and inherited $m$--$n$ margins differ by $e_m(x)$. If $e_m$ is constant across images, the inherited margins reproduce the full-support pairwise ranking.
\end{proposition}
\noindent\emph{Proof.}
Expand $\bar D_m$ and subtract. The seen-grade displacement is unchanged, so the margin difference is $e_m$. A constant difference preserves ranking by Proposition~\ref{prop:cal}.\hfill$\square$

In this diagnostic, inheritance error is exactly the local curvature of adaptation along the grade axis. It vanishes where the displacement is linear across the three adjacent grades; only the image-dependent part of curvature can change pairwise ranking. Section~\ref{sec:exp_structure} measures this distinction.

\subsection{Calibration and Inference}

Given a fitted CLIP adapter, TIE needs only aligned scores $S$ and $T$. After computing $S^{\mathrm{TIE}}$, we select $c^*$ as the largest feasible class-bias candidate on seen-only validation images. Feasibility requires the resulting seen-validation normalized MAE (nMAE) to be no more than the uncalibrated Adapted nMAE plus $\delta=0.005$. We then predict
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tie_prediction}
\hat y(x)=\arg\max_{k\in\mathcal Y}
\bigl[S_k^{\mathrm{TIE}}(x)+c^*\mathbf1[k\in\mathcal U]\bigr].
\end{equation}
The grade mask fixes the anchor map, and TIE adds $O(K)$ score operations per image, with no further training. TIE fixes the order; calibration then fixes the score.
