\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\label{sec:related_work}

\noindent\textbf{Visual ordinal classification.}
Ordinal methods exploit label order through soft or distributional targets~\cite{diaz2019sord,gao2017dldl}, rank-consistent outputs~\cite{cao2020coral}, probabilistic embeddings~\cite{li2021poe}, or contrastive objectives~\cite{pitawela2025cloc,lee2026conord}.
Vision--language models add a language prior over grades.
CLIP-IQA~\cite{wang2023clipiqa} uses CLIP's language priors for image-quality assessment; our focus is ordinal adaptation when some grades have no training images.
OrdinalCLIP~\cite{li2022ordinalclip} interpolates learnable rank embeddings, L2RCLIP~\cite{wang2023l2rclip} refines rank prompts and cross-modal ordering, and NumCLIP~\cite{du2024numclip} grounds numerical bins in language with ranking regularization.
Ranking-aware Adapter~\cite{yu2025rankingadapter} learns text-conditioned image ordering, while TIE transfers post-adaptation class-score displacements to grades without labeled adaptation images.
These methods shape how grades are represented while training on the grades that are available.
We ask a complementary question: how to give grades absent from adaptation the adaptation they missed, after training and across different CLIP adapters.
TIE does not interpolate rank embeddings; it transfers image-dependent score displacements from seen neighbors while keeping each missing grade's zero-shot reference.

\noindent\textbf{CLIP adaptation and generalization.}
CLIP is commonly adapted with learned prompts~\cite{zhou2022coop,zhou2022cocoop,khattak2023maple} or with feature, cache, and residual adapters~\cite{gao2024clipadapter,zhang2022tipadapter,yu2023taskres}.
TaskRes learns classifier residuals during adaptation; TIE operates on image-dependent score displacements after adaptation.
KgCoOp~\cite{yao2023kgcoop} regularizes learned prompt embeddings toward hand-crafted ones, whereas ProGrad~\cite{zhu2023prograd} constrains prompt updates using gradients aligned with zero-shot predictions.
Base-to-novel benchmarks, however, treat novel classes as nominal and usually evaluate them apart from the base classes.
Missing grades differ on both counts: they are predicted jointly with the seen grades, and they have adapted neighbors whose displacements can be inherited.
Robust fine-tuning likewise protects pretrained knowledge, by preserving features during adaptation~\cite{kumar2022lpft} or by interpolating zero-shot and fine-tuned weights~\cite{wortsman2022robust}.
Its output-space counterpart, blending zero-shot and adapted scores, serves as our structure-agnostic control.
Blending rescales the adaptation of every grade; TIE changes where the missing grades' adaptation comes from.

\noindent\textbf{Calibration for unseen classes.}
Post-hoc calibration rescales logits with a shared temperature~\cite{guo2017calibration} or shifts them with class-wise offsets, as in calibrated stacking for generalized zero-shot learning~\cite{chao2016gzsl} and logit adjustment for long-tailed recognition~\cite{menon2021logit}.
For classes absent during fine-tuning, Mai et al.~\cite{mai2024fine} trace the accuracy drop to a logit-scale discrepancy rather than forgotten features, and show that calibration restores the predictions.
We study a property that these corrections cannot reach: how images are ranked by the margin between a missing grade and its seen neighbor.
Class-wise offsets move decision boundaries, while neither they nor a shared positive temperature change this fixed-pair image ranking (Sec.~\ref{sec:calibration_ranking}).
TIE acts on the displacement before calibration, making ordinal transfer and operating-point selection two separate steps.
